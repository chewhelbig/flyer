Shame as the
Last Human Frontier

What AI cannot provide, and what therapists must not forget

Nicole Chew-Helbig

Gestalt Singapore · forthcoming, European Journal of Psychotherapy & Counselling

The argument

The thing we cannot name
is shame.

And we have spent forty years building a profession that is very good at avoiding it.

What the literature says instead

Alliance

Embodiment

Presence

Placeholders, not answers.

We cannot protect a core we have never named.

One word, three phenomena
1

Shame

The moral pain of being seen. Before action, before language. The signal that keeps us human.

2

Converted shame

Shame never met, never held. A felt signal calcified into a fixed state: I am defective.

3

Humiliation

Shame weaponised, displaced onto those with less power to refuse. One direction only.

The second and third arise because the first is left undefined.

Tronick, 1978 · The Still Face

The mother plays normally with her infant. Then she is asked to hold a still, expressionless face. The infant searches, reaches, protests — and then gives up.

What did I do to lose her?

Not a belief. A body. Months before the literature says it is possible.

Still Face paradigm · Tronick, Als, Adamson, Wise & Brazelton (1978)

Where the argument usually goes wrong

The limit is not that it cannot understand us.

It cannot be wounded by us.

It cannot misattune and then have to repair. It cannot be changed by having met us. It has nothing at stake.

A Tuesday afternoon
Client says “I feel disgusting for wanting this.”

The reflex

  • Anyone would feel that way.
  • You’ve done nothing wrong.
  • You should accept yourself.

The risk

  • What happens in you as you imagine me hearing that?
  • (nothing at all)
  • Yes. I can see how frightened you are.

The first reduces the distress. The second deepens the contact.

The difference

Reassurance restores composure.

Presence allows reorganisation.

Not therapy without boundaries. Therapy without guarantees.

I asked it how it could do this work

“I don’t feel the session the way you did in the room… I’m just a good mirror for it. It’s not the same as a colleague who was there — worth keeping that distinction, so I stay a sounding board and you stay the clinician.”

Unprompted. And entirely correct.

Saying it cost nothing.

What we have backwards

We build resilience against shame.

But shame is the felt signal of being in relationship — the alarm that sounds when the relationship is at risk.

So we are building resilience against
the signal of human connection.

The shame paradox

The defences we build against shame
manufacture the suffering
we then blame on shame itself.

Pain managed so that the insult can continue.

Thank you
