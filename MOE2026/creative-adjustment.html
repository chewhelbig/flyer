Principle 4 of 5

Creative Adjustment

A child's survival mechanism.

What it is

For Perls, Hefferline and Goodman, the self forms and heals at the contact boundary, where person and environment meet. Creative adjustment is the organism's ongoing way of finding the best possible contact with its environment in order to keep its balance. A child in an unsafe, rigid or rejecting field develops strategies — withdrawal, vigilance, pleasing, defiance, numbing — that were genuinely intelligent solutions at the time. The difficulty is that they become fixed, and outlive the situation that once needed them.

Why it matters for school counsellors

This reframes "problem behaviour" as survival intelligence. The anxious perfectionist, the class clown, the shut-down child — each found a way to cope with their field. Honouring the adjustment, rather than simply trying to remove it, respects the child and opens the door to gentler, more lasting change. As Perls put it, therapy aims at re-owning parts of the self, not pathologising them.

In the room

Curiosity in place of correction: "This must have helped you once — what did it protect you from?" Helping a student notice that an old, fixed adjustment may no longer fit their present field, and that new options are now available to them.

Five key points

  1. 1 Symptoms are creative solutions. The organism creatively adjusts to its environment to keep its equilibrium; what looks like a problem was once an intelligent adaptation.
  2. 2 A response, not a swallowed belief. A creative adjustment is active and contactful — the opposite of an introject, which is a belief “swallowed whole, without chewing.”
  3. 3 Adaptation to a difficult field. When the environment is rigid or rejecting, the child adjusts — withdrawing, scanning, pleasing, shutting down — to maintain organismic balance.
  4. 4 What once protected can later disconnect. Once-necessary strategies become fixed and outlive the situation that needed them, re-enacted in present relationships.
  5. 5 Witness, don't pathologise. Stay with what is, at the contact boundary; awareness lets the old adjustment reveal its meaning and frees new choices — re-owning parts of the self.

Source: Perls, Hefferline & Goodman, Gestalt Therapy: Excitement and Growth in the Human Personality (1951).

Try this

Take a behaviour you find difficult in a student. Ask: what might this have been a creative solution to? How does it make sense as survival? Let that change how you meet them next.
